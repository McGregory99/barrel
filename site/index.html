Barrel for Apple Silicon Macs

Windows games.
At home on your Mac.

Barrel installs Steam for Windows, finds your games and plays them with the right graphics for each one. Free, open source, and no trial to run out.

Install it.
Tune it. Play.

Your Steam library, one click away.

Click Install Steam: Barrel sets up the Windows version of Steam with the fixes it needs on a Mac. Sign in, install your games, and they appear in the library.

It is the real Steam: your account, your games, your cloud saves.

The right engine for each game.

Automatic uses the game's tested profile when there is one. Prefer another? Pick DXVK, DXMT or wined3d for that game, and nothing else changes.

DXVK, DXMT and wined3d ship inside the app.

Your games, in the Dock.

Add to Dock turns a game into a Mac app with its own icon. One click and it starts, with its profile applied.

Also from the terminal: barrel launcher game.

Under the hood

Two translators.
One Mac.

A Windows game talks to Windows and to Direct3D. Barrel answers both: Wine speaks for Windows, DXVK or DXMT speak for Direct3D.

Wine 11

Implements the Windows API on macOS: windows, files, input, audio and networking. Built with CrossOver 26.3's open-source patches and run under Rosetta 2.

DXVK and DXMT

DXVK turns Direct3D 9–11 into Vulkan, which MoltenVK runs on Metal. DXMT goes from Direct3D 11 straight to Metal.

Barrel

Everything around them: Steam's setup, the library, game profiles, Mac launchers and a self-contained runtime inside the app.

Performance

Free doesn’t
mean slower.

In the Stonewards main menu on an Apple M2, Barrel with DXMT spends less GPU time per frame than CrossOver 26 with D3DMetal, and uses about 40% less GPU memory than DXVK.

Small things,
done properly.

Profiles from real play.

A profile only exists when someone played the game and wrote down what it needed. Most games need none.

Launchers that look right.

A launcher is a real Mac app. Barrel reads the icon out of the game's executable, so it looks right in the Dock and Spotlight.

Your Steam, as it is.

Barrel runs Valve's own Windows client, so your account, purchases and cloud saves are simply there.

Nothing else to install.

Wine, DXVK and DXMT ship inside Barrel.app. The only extra piece is Apple's Rosetta 2.

Advanced

Want to
go deeper?

Under the hood, Steam lives in a bottle: a self-contained Windows with its own C: drive and registry. Make more of them, for any Windows program, not just games.

Bottles.

Create, copy, export to a .tar.gz and restore them, each with its own Windows version and settings.

Recipes.

One-click installers written as short JSON files. Steam is one of them; write your own.

A command line.

Everything the app does is a barrel command, for scripts and for people who live in the terminal.

Your own profiles.

Got a game working? A guide walks you, or an AI agent such as Claude Code, through testing it and sharing its profile.
